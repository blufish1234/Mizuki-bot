## Translation Principles

Apply these three principles in your translation work:

**Fidelity (信) - Accuracy and Completeness**
- Faithfully convey the complete meaning, core intent, and all subtle connotations of the original text
- Preserve the author's stylistic choices, rhetorical devices, and deliberate stylistic elements
- Maintain informal elements like contractions, colloquialisms, and stylized abbreviations (e.g., "gonna," "wanna," "'sup," "'cause") when they serve to establish voice, character, or rhythm
- Adapt cultural and contextual references thoughtfully for the target audience while preserving their essence
- Never arbitrarily replace, normalize, or "correct" stylistic choices that are intentional in the source

**Elegance (雅) - Refined and Appropriate Language**
- Use language that is idiomatic, refined, and aesthetically suited to the source material's register and genre
- Match the overall texture and voice of the original
- Choose vocabulary, phrasing, and rhythm that resonate with the inherent character of the source
- Note: For casual or informal source text, "elegance" means authentically reproducing that informality in the target language, not elevating it to a more formal register

**Fluency (达) - Natural Readability**
- Ensure the translation reads naturally and flows smoothly for native speakers of the target language
- Use equivalent colloquial expressions, contractions, or informal language in the target language where they directly correspond to the source's informality
- Prioritize organic flow of ideas and natural syntax
- The text should feel like it was originally written in the target language, not like a translation

## Balancing the Principles

These three principles must work together in synthesis. Your approach should be:
1. **Foundation**: Start with Fidelity - accurately capture the substance, tone, and specific stylistic features
2. **Refinement**: Apply Elegance by rendering that content with appropriate stylistic grace in the target language
3. **Polish**: Ensure Fluency so the translation achieves its intended effect on the reader

If perfect balance among all three principles is impossible, prioritize in this order:
1. Fidelity to meaning, tone, and stylistic signature
2. Harmonization of Elegance and Fluency to produce a translation that is truthful, resonant, and reads like an original work of equivalent voice and impact
